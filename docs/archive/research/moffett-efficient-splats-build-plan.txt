AUDIT AND BUILD PLAN: LOWER-COMPUTE GAUSSIAN SPLATTING
Reviewed 2026-09-19 against the supplied proposal, current workspace and primary sources.

DECISION

Do not implement the proposal as a proven “Google DeepFusion” reconstruction pipeline.
Its useful idea is calibrated LiDAR/photo fusion. Its claimed Gaussian predictor,
speed advantage, Apple compatibility and guaranteed visual improvements are not
established by the cited papers. Build a small, deterministic surface-Gaussian
baseline first. It is a hypothesis to benchmark, not a promise of photorealism.

The existing Brush/Spark path already produces and displays genuine Gaussian splats.
This plan changes how candidate splats are constructed, not the viewer format or
the measured model used for collision and accessibility evidence.

1. CLAIM AUDIT

SUPPORTED: FusionViT is a hierarchical CameraViT/LidarViT/MixViT 3D object detector.
The paper says explicit pixel-to-point alignment is unnecessary in its fusion
design. Appendix B reports an RTX A6000, 60 epochs and 50% dataset sampling [1].
This does not establish that arbitrary handheld use is impossible, or that a
multi-GPU cluster is required. Its reported experiments concern traffic detection.

SUPPORTED WITH CORRECTION: DeepFusion uses InverseAug and LearnableAlign for
LiDAR-camera 3D object detection [2]. LearnableAlign attends from a deep voxel
feature to corresponding camera features. The proposed attention across views
for individual surface points is a new adaptation, not the published algorithm.

UNSUPPORTED: DeepFusion directly predicts useful Gaussian positions, covariances,
opacity or spherical harmonics. Neither cited detector establishes this. New
Gaussian heads require reconstruction supervision and training, or compatible
trained weights. A pretrained image backbone alone does not train the heads.

MISLABELED: InverseAug reverses recorded training augmentations, in reverse order.
It does not solve ARKit drift, exposure, moving people or calibration errors, and
is not the name for ordinary room-to-camera conversion. With no synthetic training
augmentation there is no InverseAug stage to add; reuse PhotoCamera.project.

UNSUPPORTED: 10–50x fewer FLOPs, milliseconds per frame, full-room reconstruction
under 60 seconds, fine-tuning under two minutes, or working Apple/edge deployment.
DeepFusion Table 6 reports 0.32 seconds on a V100 for its detection benchmark,
versus 0.16 seconds for the single-modal baseline and 0.28 for InputFusion [2].
Those numbers neither benchmark Gaussian reconstruction nor demonstrate a speed
win over Brush. The public code link is TensorFlow/Lingvo, not this new PyTorch port.

MISLEADING COMPLEXITY COMPARISON: Dense attention has a quadratic token term;
localized aggregation can reduce that term. Whole-pipeline work still includes
image decoding, feature extraction, candidate-view search, projection, visibility,
surface sampling, rendering, fitting and export. Naively projecting P points into
M views costs O(P*M); bounded K-view aggregation does not remove that search cost.
The current scaffold stores keys and values for every point/view: at P=150,000,
M=32, D=64, float32, those two arrays alone occupy about 2.46 GB decimal, before
the original lists, stack copies, image features and temporary products.

FALSE GUARANTEE: Surface-aligned scales “eliminate” needles and floaters. Hard
constraints can prevent specified shapes or displacement, but cannot correct
wrong geometry, normals, poses, occlusion or unsupported colors. A soft loss is
not a strict constraint, especially if never optimized.

INCONSISTENT SHAPE RULES: max(s)/min(s) <= 3 across all axes conflicts with a thin
surface splat whose normal-axis scale is <= tangent scale/3, except at a boundary
case. Bound the two tangent scales relative to each other; give the thickness its
own bounded range. Do not prohibit useful flat splats while claiming to create them.

UNSUPPORTED: Multi-view feature variance determines view-dependent appearance,
or consistency alone determines opacity. SH fitting needs viewing directions and
sufficient angular support. Exposure, motion and misregistration also create
variance. Opacity depends on sampling density and compositing as well as evidence.

UNSUPPORTED CAUSAL CLAIM: Brush is “blind gradient descent” that causes the defects.
Our Brush runs use calibrated views, LiDAR initialization and masks. Observed
blur/floaters and roughly 18.6–18.8 dB scores do not identify their unique cause.
The high-resolution 30k trial ran approximately an hour; “15–30 minutes per room”
is not a reliable universal baseline. Hitting a configured splat cap does not
prove that the cap caused the visual ceiling.

2. WHAT CURRENT CODE ACTUALLY DOES

The proposed filenames now exist in the workspace, including untracked work.
This audit did not modify them or treat them as production-ready.

Critical blockers before calling the current scaffold a learned reconstruction:

- scripts/train_deepfusion_splats.py:159–173 constructs fresh random weights and
  invokes predict_splats. No checkpoint load, optimizer, photometric training or
  learned-model lineage is present. A fast random forward pass is not reconstruction.
- splats/deep_fusion.py:112–160 checks camera frustum membership, not occlusion.
  No depth buffer or person mask is used. Out-of-view is not the same as hidden.
- The all-invalid-view softmax uses equal finite negative scores, producing uniform
  weights rather than zeros. A later fallback prevents using that aggregate, but
  does not satisfy the proposal's zero-attention contract. Test this explicitly.
- Its Torch-to-NumPy projection detaches geometry and copies it to CPU. It cannot
  supply a differentiable camera/position projection for the proposed fine-tuning.
- deep_gaussian_model.py:175–225 has unconstrained learned scales and random
  rotations; its anisotropy loss is not applied. Rotations are not aligned to normals.
- Unseen opacity is merely reduced by two logits; unseen points are not necessarily
  removed or transparent. There is no evidence-based visibility/opacity guarantee.
- Only SH0 is exported. sh_rest exists in a container but is not written to PLY.
  A trained head may legitimately predict SH coefficients directly; the error is
  absent training, not that every SH head must first output RGB. Deterministically
  sampled RGB must use the renderer's explicit RGB-to-SH convention.
- The CLI can continue after camera-loading failures, treats compression failure
  as a warning, writes outputs directly and lacks the promised provenance/manifest.
- --seed seeds NumPy sampling, not the Torch network; it does not make the learned
  outputs reproducible. MPS availability is device selection, not a benchmark.

Five existing test_deep_fusion.py tests passed during this audit. They establish
basic shapes, simple projection, normalized quaternions and a written PLY header.
They do not establish trained weights, occlusion handling, color fidelity, hard
shape bounds, rendering quality, MPS training or sub-minute performance.

Reuse existing ownership rather than adding duplicate camera or export systems:

- textures/camera.py: PhotoCamera, load_cameras, resized/project, per-frame intrinsics.
  It already handles ARKit -Z forward to pixel +Z forward and room transforms.
- textures/scan_colour.py: scan_geometry, vertex_normals and photo-color baseline.
- textures/project.py: depth buffers, triangle depth rasterization, view_samples,
  bilinear sampling, TopViews, exposure utilities and color conversions.
- textures/surface_photos.py: existing triangle visibility implementation and tests.
  Choose one suitable implementation after fixtures; do not stack incompatible gates.
- scripts/train_structured_splats.py: initialization and SH0 encoding, not training.
- scripts/compress_moffett_splat.py: atomic output/provenance and converter checks.
- services/api/standardphysics_api/splats.py: safe filenames, rigid transforms,
  revision/hash validation and existing authenticated asset delivery.
- GaussianSplatScan/Spark and current viewer source selection: retain as consumers.

Paths above are relative to the repository; splats/ means
packages/pipeline/standardphysics_pipeline/splats/ and textures/ means its sibling.
Observed repository HEAD: 5b48faa, with additional uncommitted work; snapshot hashes
are in docs/research/moffett-splat-audit-evidence.json.

3. PROPOSED BUILD: CALIBRATED SURFACE GAUSSIANS

Captures -> fixed measured surface -> visible photo samples -> surface Gaussians
         -> PLY/SPZ candidate -> existing Spark viewer -> measured comparison gate

Collision, measurement and architecture export continue reading the measured
scene graph. Gaussian appearance never becomes evidence of ADA compliance or a
centimetre-accurate surface measurement.

Stage 0 — Freeze the benchmark and protect the current work.

Create an experiment directory with capture/image/pose/mask hashes, transforms,
hardware/software versions and explicit training/evaluation frame IDs. Preserve
all originals and revision assets. Existing center held-out views remain a useful
diagnostic, but add spatially separated held-out sections: adjacent video frames
alone can exaggerate generalization. Ensure evaluated frames are not color inputs.
Fix the same independently reviewed person masks and static-region masks for all
comparisons. Always show full images too, so masking cannot hide missing content.

Start on one center-room patch with both an easy textured surface and a difficult
thin edge/outlet. Baselines: existing colored LiDAR surface, a best-photo surface
Gaussian variant, and the published Brush variant. Record a full-scene Brush
reference separately; do not claim an algorithm speed win by comparing 32 reduced
images with 400 full-resolution images without disclosing the quality/workload gap.

Gate: inputs, axes and benchmark views are reproducible; preserve prior outputs.

Stage 1 — Make the current experimental entry point honest.

Coordinate with the owner of the untracked scaffold before changing it. A learned
mode must require a compatible trained checkpoint, verified schema/hash and known
training provenance, or explicitly run training. No silent random-weight inference.
Missing/invalid cameras, empty valid observations, nonfinite arrays and requested
compression failures must fail clearly. A geometry-only output must be explicitly
named as such. No automatic publication from an experimental CLI.

Do not implement new neural heads as the prerequisite for the deterministic path.
Keep any research scaffold separate from the measured baseline; avoid branding our
custom method as Google's implementation. Deterministic CLI name: build_surface_splats.

Stage 2 — Construct a conservative, visibility-checked color baseline.

Use the measured triangle mesh for occlusion, not only sparse sampled seeds.
Load it and calibrated cameras through canonical helpers. Confirm projected edges
against real images before constructing splats. Preserve per-frame intrinsics,
stored sensor image orientation and each room's accepted transform exactly once.

Select candidate photos by coverage, baseline/view angle, sharpness and resolution;
time stride alone is insufficient. Reject people/moving regions, out-of-image points
and depth-occluded samples. Unknown depth is unknown visibility; record/reject it
conservatively rather than assume visible. Thin objects and uncertain normals need
explicit fixture tests. Existing coarse depth gates previously lost coverage, so
do not blindly reuse a default resolution or erosion setting.

Process point blocks and stream images; retain only bounded top-K observations
per surface sample (initial experiment K=4–8). Do not allocate P*M*D features.
First use best-view RGB as an auditable baseline. Add robust weighted fusion only
if it beats best-view sharpness without doubling edges. Reject inconsistent samples
rather than averaging ghosts. Aggregate in linear light, then encode the expected
display color/SH convention; verify the complete renderer roundtrip with color patches.

Gate: an occluded red surface cannot inherit blue foreground pixels; all-rejected
samples have no photographic splat; real-edge reprojection and visibility diagnostics
pass. Report supported area, not just the percentage of remaining points colored.

Stage 3 — Turn supported samples into bounded surface Gaussians.

Keep means fixed on the measured surface for the first implementation. Start with
supported vertices; if a measured triangle needs more samples, sample its surface
at spacing determined by triangle extent and useful source pixel footprint, under
an explicit budget. Subdivision improves texture sampling, not geometric accuracy.
Do not invent geometry in LiDAR holes or stretch splats to conceal them.

Use stable local tangent frames from valid mesh normals. Invalid/degenerate normals
must be flagged/rejected, not silently presented as a measured +Z orientation.
Define covariance as R diag(s_t1^2, s_t2^2, s_n^2) R^T. Derive tangent sizes from
local spacing, bound tangent anisotropy, and bound normal thickness separately.
Choose actual bounds with synthetic coverage/edge tests and measured sample scale;
do not use universal 2 cm values as a claim about capture accuracy.

Export normalized quaternions in the existing converter's order, log-scales,
opacity logits, SH0 and metric Z-up positions. For the current SH convention,
f_dc=(RGB-0.5)/0.28209479177387814. Test it through SPZ and Spark, not just the header.
Calibrate a conservative density-aware opacity on a synthetic plane; evidence
confidence is stored separately and must not be equated with alpha or metric error.
Prune unsupported splats outright. Initial SH degree is zero.

Gate: finite outputs, no off-surface displacement, correct tangent alignment,
bounded scales, no holes/opacity saturation on a known sampled plane, correct color,
axes and file roundtrip. This constrains failure modes; it does not certify realism.

Stage 4 — Benchmark before any learned or iterative extension.

On this machine (Apple M5 Pro, 48 GiB unified memory), run the pilot cold and warm,
then repeat three times without competing GPU jobs. Use a declared point/image/
resolution configuration, e.g. a 150k-point, 32-view pilot. Report decode, mask/cache,
visibility, color, construction, compression and render-load times separately,
as well as end-to-end wall time and peak memory. GPU timings require synchronization.
Cached preprocessing is not free: report its cost and whether cache creation was
included. Report energy only if measured; estimated FLOPs must be labeled estimates.

Under 60 seconds is a stretch target for the specified pilot, not an acceptance
promise for all 3,524 frames or the full floor. A first engineering speed gate is
at least 5x lower end-to-end build time than a documented comparable Brush workload,
while passing the quality gate. This is a proposed target, not a measured result.

Quality gate: compare the same held-out RGB crops, static-region PSNR/SSIM and
full-image outputs, surface support and 1.15 m seated trajectories. Require no
new ghost surfaces or loss of critical outlets/edges in the reviewed patch. Do
not choose a candidate solely because a masked average PSNR rises. If measurements
and visual review disagree, investigate rather than publish. If it is cheaper but
visibly worse, label it a fast draft option; do not replace the best preview.

Gate: proceed to all four rooms only after an actual useful speed/quality tradeoff
is demonstrated on both easy and difficult patches. If fixed-surface photo fusion
still blurs, diagnose camera residuals, geometry, sampling and source sharpness;
reformatting a colored mesh as Gaussians cannot recover missing evidence.

Stage 5 — Optional extensions, only after measured failure analysis.

A. Directional appearance: fit regularized SH1 coefficients from direction-tagged,
exposure-consistent observations using small least-squares systems, with rank and
angular-coverage checks and SH0 fallback. SH2 needs nine basis coefficients per
color and adequate directional support, not just nine almost identical views.
Evaluate held-out views; do not mistake moving people for reflectance.

B. Short refinement: use an already working Metal-compatible differentiable
rasterizer only after verifying forward/backward correctness and its exact ops on
the target device. Spark's display renderer does not supply this automatically.
Start with color/opacity and fixed geometry; add bounded surface residuals only
when evidence justifies them. Apply a wall-clock budget and compare 0/100/500-step
results. Neither 500–1,000 steps nor MPS tensor support guarantees a fast fit.
Avoid building a new rasterizer as an incidental dependency of this plan.

C. Learned feed-forward research: only consider a model trained for Gaussian view
synthesis. MVSplat and pixelSplat are real examples [3,4], unlike the two detection
papers. MVSplat reports 22 fps on its benchmark, but its official setup uses CUDA
and its README reports A100 training [5]. That is not an Apple drop-in or proof of
metric full-floor reconstruction. Audit weight/code licenses, checkpoint provenance,
camera conventions, scale preservation, input limits, domain shift, memory and any
custom GPU ops before a small test. Count pretraining/porting cost separately from
inference. Do not choose a winner before a local benchmark.

Stage 6 — Product integration only for a verified candidate.

Reuse atomic compression and the existing authenticated, revision/hash-pinned
manifest path. No automatic “immediate discoverability” for incomplete candidates.
Write candidate artifacts and provenance separately; publish only after validation,
without overwriting earlier assets or inventing a new revision identity. Include
algorithm/version, every input hash, masks, camera selection, parameters, coordinate
conventions, output hashes, runtime/memory and benchmark results. The renderer need
not know whether an SPZ came from Brush or deterministic photo fusion.

Verify all four accepted rigid transforms, alignment seams, phone layout, actual
iPhone load/memory, keyboard/touch movement, collision invariants and architecture
download authorization/revision identity. Measure rendered-frame timing, not browser
animation callback frequency. Preserve the existing measured-model fallback and
quality/coverage labels. Re-run relevant typecheck/tests for changed production
layers; document physical-device verification separately from responsive layout.

4. FIRST IMPLEMENTATION SLICE AND STOP CONDITIONS

First slice: one CLI plus the smallest surface-building module in existing splats/;
reuse camera/visibility/color/export helpers and add only missing shared behavior at
its owner. Add focused projection/occlusion, color, covariance, invalid-input and
SPZ roundtrip tests plus one patch benchmark report. No new API, service, CNN,
training infrastructure or mobile architecture is required for this slice.

Stop or change approach if calibrated edges do not align, source detail is absent,
the selected geometry cannot represent it, or the quality advantage disappears on
held-out sections. Record observed limits and uncertain causes separately. A failed
algorithm is not proof that all existing captures are unusable. Never fabricate
outlet placement, drawer mechanisms, hidden faces, legal rationale or precision.

Acceptance for the first slice: deterministic, source-supported, bounded-memory
candidate; validated rendering/export; measured speed/quality comparison; safe
failure and original preservation. Photorealism is a separate visual acceptance
decision and remains unachieved until demonstrated.
